ALBANIA TRAVEL GUIDE

Guide to Sarandë: Ksamil, Butrint, Corfu & Tirana

By Carl Tomich | Last reviewed August 2, 2026 | Editorial policy

Sarandë is the easiest base in southern Albania: cheap local buses, clear-water beach days, ancient ruins, and simple day trips that actually work.

Why Sarandë Works as a Base

Albania has rough edges in a good way. It feels lived in, not polished for tourists. If you stay in Sarandë, everything gets simple: you can go beach, history, and even international day trip mode without heavy planning.

You settle into a rhythm fast: slow morning coffee on the promenade, local bus for a half-day mission, back before sunset, then dinner by the water. It doesn’t feel like checklist travel. It feels like you actually lived somewhere.

Realistic Monthly Budget in Sarandë

  • Rent: €340 (Airbnb, off-season)
  • Power: €50
  • Food: €400–450
  • Transport: €10–15
  • Phone: €15

Total: around €800–900/month. That’s not extreme budget mode either. You can eat out, move around, and still keep costs steady.

Getting to Ksamil and Butrint (The Easy Way)

If you’re based in Sarandë, skip the tour logistics. The local bus is the best option and the cheapest.

Where to catch it

  • Go to the main road near the waterfront promenade.
  • Look for signs saying Ksamil or Butrint.
  • No formal station needed. If unsure, ask locals “Ksamil?” and they’ll point you.

Cost and timing

  • 200 lek per trip (about €2), paid onboard.
  • ~20 minutes to Ksamil.
  • ~30 minutes to Butrint National Park.
  • Frequent buses during the day, no booking stress.

The ride is scenic the whole way: coastline, elevated sea views, and moments where you’ll want to put your phone down and just look outside.

Ksamil: Clear Water, No Effort

Get off in central Ksamil and you’re usually 2–5 minutes from the beach. Water is clear, everything is close together, and it’s easy to explore without overplanning.

Butrint: Ancient Site With Zero Transport Hassle

Stay on the same bus route until the final stop and you’ll be dropped at the entrance to Butrint National Park. Entry is usually around €10, and you can walk straight into the ruins.

Sarandë Highlights You Shouldn’t Skip

Lëkurësi Castle at Sunset

Walk, taxi, or drive to Lëkurësi Castle for the best panorama over Sarandë and across to Corfu. There’s a restaurant and open terrace at the top, and sunset is the standout timing.

The Promenade at Night

Simple and relaxed: evening walks, waterfront restaurants, people out by the sea. It’s not overly polished, which is exactly why it feels good.

Corfu Day Trip (Easy Contrast)

From Sarandë, you can take a ferry to Corfu in roughly 30 minutes. Return fares often start around €50 depending on season and operator. Corfu feels more structured and polished, and that contrast makes you appreciate the Albanian coast even more when you return.

Tirana for Context

Tirana gives you a different side of Albania: faster pace, more urban energy, and deeper historical context through spots like Bunk’Art. It’s worth adding if you want to understand the country beyond the coast.

Getting to Sarandë from Athens

One of the easiest overland options is the direct bus from Athens, typically around 8 hours and roughly €35, including the border crossing process. If you want a step-by-step breakdown, read my full post: How to Get to Sarandë from Athens by Bus.

Final Thoughts

Sarandë gives you the base. Ksamil gives you the water. Butrint gives you history. Corfu gives you contrast. Tirana gives you context.

Put together, Albania becomes one of the easiest destinations in Europe to actually enjoy without overpaying or overplanning.
