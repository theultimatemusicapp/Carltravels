Living in Bangkok

Bangkok is a sensory overload. It’s a city where 500-year-old temples sit in the shadow of neon-lit skyscrapers, and where you can buy a Michelin-star meal on a plastic stool for $2. For a digital nomad or expat, it remains one of the most compelling places on earth to call home.

Why Bangkok in 2025?

While other hubs have seen prices skyrocket and infrastructure lag, Bangkok has doubled down on its status as a creative capital. The introduction of the Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) has been a literal game-changer, allowing remote workers to stay for up to 5 years with multiple entries.

But beyond the visas, it’s the ease of life. You can decide you want a world-class pizza at 3 AM and have it delivered by a Grab driver in 20 minutes. You can join a community of thousands of creators at any one of the hundreds of co-working spaces.

The Neighborhood Breakdown

Sukhumvit (Asok / Phrom Phong / Thong Lo)

The expat heart. High-end condos, international bars, and incredible nightlife. It's expensive by Thai standards but very convenient.

Ari

The "hipster" neighborhood. Residential, leafy, and filled with boutique cafes. It feels more "local" than Sukhumvit while still being connected via the BTS.

Sathorn / Silom

The financial district. Great for those who want a mix of high-end bars, green spaces (Lumphini Park), and a professional atmosphere.

Cost of Living: The Reality

Is it still cheap? Yes and no. If you want to live exactly like you do in London or Sydney, you'll pay Western prices. But if you embrace the local systems, your money goes incredibly far.

  • Housing: $500 gets you a clean, modern studio with a pool and gym near the BTS. $1200 gets you luxury.
  • Food: $300 a month is plenty if you eat Thai food. $800 if you want western brunch and craft beer every night.
  • Transport: The BTS/MRT (trains) are world-class and cheap ($1-$2 per trip). Use Grab or Bolt for taxis.

Carl's Verdict: Bangkok vs Hanoi

"I love Hanoi for its grit, its history, and its coffee culture. But Bangkok wins on pure efficiency. If you need to scale a business, if you need fast internet, and if you want a city that actually *works*, Bangkok is the place. It's less 'authentic' than Hanoi in parts, but it's far easier to live in long-term."

Final Advice: Get a Scooter?

Unless you are extremely confident on two wheels, don't. Bangkok traffic is some of the most congested and unpredictable in the world. Stick to the trains and the occasional "Win" (motorcycle taxi) for short distances when you're in a rush.
