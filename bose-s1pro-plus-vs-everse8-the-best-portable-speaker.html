Bose S1 Pro Plus vs. Everse 8: Portable PA Showdown

Welcome back to Carl Tomich Tech Reviews! Today, we’re diving into audio gear with a head-to-head comparison of two top portable PA speakers: the Bose S1 Pro Plus and the Electro-Voice Everse 8. These battery-powered systems are perfect for busking, solo gigs, small events, or casual jam sessions in the park. I’ve tested both on the streets of Melbourne and at outdoor venues in Bali, and I’m here to help you decide which one suits your needs.

Great visuals are only half the story—audio quality can make or break your performance. Let’s break down the size, features, sound, and value of these two contenders.

Why These Speakers?

The Bose S1 Pro Plus and Everse 8 are compact, battery-powered PA systems designed for portability and performance. Whether you’re a busker, solo musician, or event host, these speakers deliver powerful sound in a travel-friendly package.

Specs at a Glance

Feature Bose S1 Pro Plus Everse 8
Woofer Size 6.5-inch 8-inch
Battery Life Up to 11 hours Up to 12 hours
Inputs 2x XLR/TRS, 3.5mm aux 2x XLR/TRS, 1/4-inch aux
Wireless Bluetooth 5.0, wireless transmitters Bluetooth 5.0
Weight 6.5 kg 7.6 kg
Price (AUD) ~$1,300 ~$900–$1,000

Speaker Size: Does Bigger Mean Better?

The Everse 8 boasts an 8-inch woofer, while the Bose S1 Pro Plus has a 6.5-inch driver. Larger drivers typically deliver deeper bass and higher volume, but Bose’s engineering optimizes clarity and dispersion. In my tests, the Everse 8 had a punchier low-end, ideal for backing tracks or DJ sets, while the Bose excelled in vocal and midrange clarity for solo acoustic performances.

Inputs & Features: Aux Wars

Both speakers offer dual combo XLR/TRS inputs for mics or instruments, but their aux inputs differ:

  • Bose S1 Pro Plus: 3.5mm aux (great for phones or tablets)
  • Everse 8: 1/4-inch aux (better for mixers or guitars)

This small detail matters—choose based on your gear to avoid adapter hassles. Both also support Bluetooth 5.0 for wireless streaming, but the Bose includes wireless transmitter support for mics or instruments, a huge plus for cable-free performances.

Battery Life & Portability

Both are battery-powered for cordless gigs:

  • Bose S1 Pro Plus: Up to 11 hours, 6.5 kg, sleek design
  • Everse 8: Up to 12 hours, 7.6 kg, slightly bulkier

The Bose is easier to carry for street performances, while the Everse 8’s extra heft supports its beefier sound. Both lasted through 4–5 hour busking sessions in my tests without needing a recharge.

Wireless Wins?

The Bose S1 Pro Plus includes wireless transmitters that slot into the speaker, allowing cable-free mic or instrument connections. This premium feature justifies part of its higher price and is a game-changer for performers who value mobility. The Everse 8 relies on Bluetooth for audio streaming but lacks dedicated wireless transmitter support.

Sound Test: Guitar, Vocals, Bass

I A/B tested both speakers with guitar, vocals, and bass tracks under identical conditions. The Everse 8 delivers deeper bass, making it ideal for genres like EDM or hip-hop, while the Bose S1 Pro Plus shines with clear mids and vocals, perfect for acoustic sets.

Pros

Bose S1 Pro Plus

  • Ultra-portable at 6.5 kg
  • Wireless transmitters for cable-free performance
  • Clear mids and vocals, ideal for acoustic gigs
  • 3.5mm aux for easy phone/tablet connectivity
  • Up to 11 hours battery life

Everse 8

  • Deeper bass with 8-inch woofer
  • More affordable (~$900–$1,000 AUD)
  • Up to 12 hours battery life
  • 1/4-inch aux for mixers or guitars
  • Robust build for outdoor use

Cons

Bose S1 Pro Plus

  • Higher price (~$1,300 AUD)
  • Weaker bass compared to Everse 8
  • Smaller driver limits max volume

Everse 8

  • Bulkier at 7.6 kg
  • No wireless transmitter support
  • Less clear mids for vocals

Final Thoughts

Both the Bose S1 Pro Plus and Everse 8 are excellent portable PA speakers, but your choice depends on your priorities:

  • Bose S1 Pro Plus: Ideal for solo acoustic performers who value portability and wireless convenience.
  • Everse 8: Best for those needing deeper bass and higher volume at a lower price, like DJs or backing track users.
