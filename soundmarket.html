If you upload a video with the wrong music, the outcome is predictable: YouTube flags the content, ad revenue disappears, and sometimes the audio gets muted entirely. That is exactly why music licensing platforms for YouTube creators have become essential.

How to Find Royalty Free Music for YouTube Videos

If you upload videos to YouTube regularly, you must have proper usage rights for your music. Most creators end up using one of these options:

  • The YouTube Audio Library
  • Paid platforms like Artlist or Epidemic Sound
  • Curated royalty free libraries like SoundMarket

While YouTube’s free library is useful, many creators move to dedicated platforms for better track quality and clearer monetization licensing.

What Makes SoundMarket Different

SoundMarket is a royalty free music library for creators, but the catalog sourcing is different from many traditional stock libraries. A lot of music comes from professional composers who work in film, television, advertising, and games.

Many tracks were originally created for real productions and later added to SoundMarket when they were not used in final edits. That gives the platform a less "generic stock" sound. The current library has around 18,000 tracks and is curated, which can reduce how often you hear the same songs reused across YouTube.

Features Designed for Video Creators

SoundMarket includes practical tools for editing workflows. Many songs provide ALT mixes that remove lead instruments so tracks sit better under narration or dialogue.

Higher subscription plans also include:

  • Music stems for adjusting drums, bass, and individual layers
  • Cut-down durations in 15, 30, and 60 second versions
  • Versions prepared for social and short-form content

Free Tier for Non-Monetized Channels

If your YouTube channel is not monetized, SoundMarket allows you to use tracks for free. This lowers the barrier for new creators who are still building toward monetization.

Once monetization starts, you can move to a paid plan and whitelist your channel for continued licensing coverage.

SoundMarket Pricing vs Competitors

Paid plans begin around $12.99/month, which is usually lower than common alternatives that often begin around $20 to $30/month. For creators publishing often, this can make SoundMarket easier to justify.

Testing the Music in a Real Video Edit

I tested multiple SoundMarket tracks inside a travel edit. The production quality is strong and worked well for cinematic travel sequences, documentary pacing, and voiceover-heavy scenes.

The catalog is smaller than some major competitors, but many tracks felt less overused, which helps if you want a fresher soundtrack identity.
